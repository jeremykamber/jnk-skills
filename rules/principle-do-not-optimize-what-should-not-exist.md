# Do not optimize what should not exist

**Why.** This is the most common error of a smart engineer: making something faster or cleaner that should not exist at all. Speed on the wrong thing is wasted work, and it hides the deletion you owed.

**The order, and it is binding.**

1. Question the requirement (see `rule://principle-question-the-requirement`).
2. Delete the part or the step (see `rule://principle-delete-before-you-add`).
3. Only now simplify or optimize.
4. Then accelerate.
5. Automate last, once the process is settled.

Each step done out of order invalidates the one before it. Do not dig the grave faster, and do not automate a process you have not finished deleting from.

**When it applies.** A perf task, a cleanup, an automation, a "make this faster" or "make this nicer" request, and any new machinery proposed to hold existing work.

**When it does not.** A measured regression on path that must exist. Then the ordering still holds: confirm the path should exist, delete what you can, and optimize the rest.

**Cite it.** Name the step you are on and the step you refused to skip to.
